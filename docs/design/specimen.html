1 · Colorevery token, grouped by layer. hex values and ratios are read from the linked stylesheet at load

One ink. The product is drawn in a single cobalt hue at seven steps, inherited unchanged. Body text is not a separate color from the accent: --text resolves to --ink. Everything that is not cobalt is paper, a wash, a sealed signal pigment, or an Area ink. Layer 0 is off limits to components. A component writes --ink or --paper, never --cobalt-600. Section 1.1 renders the primitives because cataloguing them is what this section is for; no other rule on this sheet reaches into that layer.

The 12-ink Area ramp is the single sealed exception to one-ink, and it exists because Areas must be read pre-attentively across roughly ninety blocks a week. Its carriers, its hatch and the list of what it may never do are section 6. An ink fill inverts the palette, and syncr has exactly one class of ink fill: a panel or dialog header. The sidebar is paper, so navigation takes no inverse treatment at all. That decision is what keeps --on-ink, --on-ink-muted and --rule-on-ink down to three tokens.

1.3 · Contrast ledger: every text pair, the ring on every surface, the signal markers, the twelve Area inks, the washes. computed at load, never asserted

Thresholds: a text pair needs 4.5:1 for AA at these sizes; a focus ring, a state rule, a status dot, an Area ink or anything marking a control boundary is a non-text UI component and needs 3:1; a hairline divider, a grid line and a wash are informational, because a tint on cream has about 1.1:1 of dynamic range and therefore never carries meaning alone. A row marked sealed clears its threshold and is still forbidden, because the seal governs which channel a pigment may use, not whether it is legible.

— rows fail their threshold, and the count in this sentence is computed from the ledger rather than typed. —
The ring is chosen by the surface it lands on, not by the element's own fill. At --state-focus-offset 2px the ring sits on the parent, so a primary button with an --ink fill keeps the standard ring: its parent is paper, and inverting it would give — instead. Only a container whose own fill is ink inverts, and it inverts once, for everything inside it. This sheet has one such surface, the dialog header in section 4, and its rule is .overlay > header :focus-visible. A wash is not an ink fill: the hover fill, the current nav item and a panel header keep the standard ring, which measures — at its worst wash.

2.2 · The two block-title tiers, rendered at the real box heights, with the arithmetic computed from the tokens

The block title is --fs-block, which resolves to --fs-chrome 11.5px: the same size as every other control and ledger row in the system. An earlier draft set it to 9.5px to keep the label floor at 15px. Rendered, it was too small to read at the size the Week screen is actually used at, and it made the product's primary content the smallest type in the system. The floor is the sum of what has to fit, not a round number, and both sums below are computed from --rule-emphasis, --block-pad-t, --fs-block, --lh-block and --hairline rather than restated. The compact tier pays for its title by giving up --block-pad-t, so the title sits against the Area rule. That is a real cost and the right trade: the alternative is no title at all, and an unlabeled Wake Up reads as a rendering fault rather than as a deliberate tier.
The title wraps to whatever fits; it does not truncate at one line. A measured ledger over one real week showed 4 of 37 distinct titles becoming ambiguous under end-ellipsis: Amazon Interview Prep and Amazon Interview Prep - Behavioral both collapse to the same string, as do Visual Computing Lab and Visual Computing Lecture. End-ellipsis discards precisely the tail that distinguishes a title from its sibling. The line count is therefore computed per block from its own height, so a clip lands on a line boundary rather than mid-glyph, and the third specimen above prints the count it derived.

Stroke, caps and joins are read from --icon-stroke, --icon-cap and --icon-join rather than restated, because the two reference sheets of the inherited language diverged once when the tokens were silent on caps. 6 · The Area ramp, and its sealthe one break with one-ink. twelve pigments, three legal carriers, hatch always on

Areas do triple duty: the wedge of the allocation model, the type of a template slot, and the category of a task. A user carries several at once and must read which Area a block belongs to pre-attentively, across roughly ninety blocks a week. Label text alone cannot do that at the fifteen-minute tier, and a tint ramp cannot either, because the whole wash channel is about 1.1:1 wide. That is the argument for the exception, and it is the only one accepted.

The 225 degree slot is deliberately vacant: that is cobalt's hue, and an Area that read as the system ink would be unreadable as a category. — Three pigments sit on the sealed signal hues by construction, which are 01 near oxide, 03 near amber and 06 near verdigris. That collision is accepted rather than avoided, because squeezing twelve pigments into the remaining 270 degrees would put the hue steps below the point where dark colors separate. The carrier rule is what disambiguates them: a signal is never a fill, an Area is only ever a fill or a rule.

Assignment order is not numeric order. Areas are dealt 01, 05, 08, 10, 03, 07, 12, 06, 02, 04, 09, 11, so a user with four Areas gets four pigments roughly ninety degrees apart. —
Hatch is always on. It is not an accessibility toggle, because color is never the only encoding: roughly one man in twelve cannot separate twelve categorical hues, and three of these pigments sit on the signal hues by construction. Each hatch is drawn in a lighter step of the wedge's own ink, so it reads as texture rather than as a second color. Six patterns is the honest ceiling: past that they stop separating at wedge scale, so a pattern repeats only between hues that are far apart. —

6.1 · The three legal carriers, exhaustively. One carrier per context, never two at once

Carrier 1 of 3 · proportional surfaces

The block's 2px top rule, and nothing else. A real border-top at --rule-emphasis, not an inset shadow, so it participates in the 19px floor's arithmetic rather than sitting on top of the title. The fill stays --paper-raised, the left rule stays reserved for state, and the circadian frame takes --frame-wash and no Area ink at all: routines are not budgeted as discretionary time, so the frame reads as structure rather than as a category competing with Fitness.

Carrier 2 of 3 · ledger surfaces

A filled chip up to 10px in the row gutter, always paired with the Area's name. On a ledger surface the name is a stronger redundancy than any hatch, so the chip carries no texture. The chip is one of the four members of the circular allowlist in section 4.

Carrier 3 of 3 · charts

Stacked bar · the same array

A pie wedge fill or a stacked bar fill, both hatched. The pie is the mental model the product exists to connect to the schedule, so it is drawn here rather than described. —

7 · The signal palette, and its sealoxide, verdigris, amber. two sanctioned uses, and no sanctioned fill at all

The signals are printer's inks rather than web primaries, so they sit beside cobalt instead of fighting it. color.css permits them in exactly two places: status and severity indicators, meaning dots, rules, glyphs and their labels, which is where the conflict block's 3px left rule lives, and the tradeoff panel's edge. The severity model pairs each signal with a position rather than with a volume of pigment, which is what keeps two uses from becoming five.

syncr sanctions no signal fill at all. The language it inherits sanctions exactly one, the amber search-match chip, and syncr has no match state to spend it on: search on the Backlog screen filters rows rather than marking ranges inside them. Zero is therefore the whole allowance, and the seal below has one fewer exception to protect than the sheet it is modelled on. Everywhere else a signal is a rule, a glyph, a dot or text.

Each signal names two roles. The marker step carries dots, rules, glyphs and washes, where 3:1 is the bar; the text step below it carries label ink, where 4.5:1 is the bar and the marker step fails. Amber has no text step: it measures — as text on page paper, so an amber indicator never colors its own label. Section 1.3 computes all of it.

8 · Motion policyzero, without exception. the inherited language kept one; syncr has nothing to spend it on

Nothing eases, fades, slides, shimmers or spins. --duration is —. There are no skeleton loaders, because a shimmer is decoration. There is no spinner, and the circular allowlist does not admit one: it holds four static circles, and a rotating element is motion.

The inherited language kept exactly one exception, and syncr takes none. That exception existed for a cold-start index measured at 4.6 seconds, where a progress bar advancing on a changed number was the only honest way to report the wait. syncr has no such wait. A solve over roughly thirty blocks a day is instantaneous, and calendar sync reports as a count that changes when a number changes, which is a discrete redraw rather than motion. An exception with nothing to protect is not inherited: it is dropped.

A drag is discrete. Dragging a block does not move the block. A hairline insertion marker snaps to the quarter hour under the cursor, and the block redraws once, on drop. Reflow of the unpinned remainder is one redraw, never an animated settle. Continuous input, discrete output.

The quarter-hour subdivision is a state change, not a flourish. At rest the grid draws the hour only, in --rule-faint. While a drag is in progress the hour steps up to --rule and the quarter-hour lines appear, because that is the one moment the user needs to see where the snap will land. It appears and disappears; it does not fade.

The drag, as its two frames · there is no third frame between them
Frame 1 · grip, and while held
The block has not moved. The marker is at the quarter hour under the cursor and prints the time it will land on. The grid has stepped up to --rule at the hour and revealed --rule-faint at the quarter.
Frame 2 · on drop
One redraw. The block is at 13:00, the marker is gone, the grid is calm again, and the block is pinned by the edit: every manual edit is a pin, and the solver reflows only the unpinned remainder around it.
Grep is the proof, not this paragraph. This file declares no transition, no animation, no will-change, no transform and no keyframes at-rule; the cross that cancels a sealed specimen is centered with a grid rather than a translate for that reason alone. —
Provenance. Every hex value and every ratio on this sheet is computed in the browser from frontend/src/tokens/ at load, so sections 1.3 and 6 cannot drift from the build, and every count stated in prose is read from the array that renders the thing being counted. Block names, times and durations come from a real reference week and are realistic rather than authoritative: the circadian frame is drawn as spans, which is what the product specifies rather than what the source calendar exported. The allocation figures are constructed, and the construction is stated where it is used: one array of minutes per Area, from which the discretionary total, the percentages, the pie, the stacked bar and both ledgers are derived. The grid arithmetic assumes --grid-h at --visible-hours, which is what a 13-inch 1440×900 display yields after the page band, summary strip and day header.
