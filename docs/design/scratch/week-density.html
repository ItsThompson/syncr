1 · The Week screen at the agreed width policya scale model of the real layout. hover, tab, and toggle the panel

The week grid absorbs surplus width. The sidebar and the detail panel are fixed. This is the inherited language's transcript rule restated, and the justification is the same: block titles in the 15 and 30 minute tiers cannot wrap, so the grid is the only column that cannot absorb a squeeze. Giving surplus width to the side columns would starve the one column that needs it. Toggle the panel to watch the day columns take the 306px back rather than distributing it.

2 · The width ledgercomputed from the tokens at load, at three viewports and both panel states

A day column's usable text width is the column, less --grid-inset twice, less --block-pad-x twice, less the 3px state rule. Characters are that width over the 11.5px mono advance of 6.9px. The floor is 17 characters, and this ledger set it. 17 is where a real title stops keeping the tail that distinguishes it from its siblings. The ledger also falsified a token: --bp-wide was 1440px by analogy with the inherited language, and at exactly 1440 with the panel open a day column yields 15 characters. Working backwards from 17 characters gives a 132.3px column, a 926px grid and a 1524px viewport, so --bp-wide is now 1536px. Rows that are not a shipped pairing are marked as such.

3 · What actually truncatesevery distinct title in the real week, measured against the real budget

Titles are not hypothetical here: these are the distinct block titles in the week rendered above. The question a density probe has to answer is not "does it fit" but "when it does not fit, is what survives still the thing you recognize?"

This ledger changed the design. Under white-space:nowrap it reported 4 of 37 titles AMBIGUOUS: Amazon Interview Prep and Amazon Interview Prep · Behavioral both collapsed to Amazon Interview P…, as did Visual Computing Lab and Visual Computing Lecture. End-ellipsis throws away exactly the tail that distinguishes a title from its sibling. Those blocks run 60 to 150 minutes, so they are 52 to 130px tall with room for several lines: the ambiguity was caused by the nowrap, not by the width. Titles now wrap to a line count computed from each block's own height. The table below therefore measures the single-line tier, which is the only tier that still truncates.

4 · The zoom ladder on a whole weekthe same seven days at 6, 12 and 16 visible hours

Section 1 of block-states.html proved the ladder on one column. The question here is different: at what zoom does the week stop being readable as a shape? 6 hours shows a quarter of a day in detail and loses the week. 16 hours shows the whole waking day and slivers the frame. 12 is the default because it holds a working day and keeps every 30 minute block labeled.

5 · The tier ladder, and reading a block you cannot readfour tiers, and the answer is the panel rather than a popover

A block below --block-h-label does not lose its title immediately. There is one degradation step first, and it exists because the three-tier ladder was rejected in review: an unlabeled Wake Up reads as a rendering fault rather than as a deliberate tier. The compact tier drops to --fs-block-compact 9.5px at line-height 1 and gives up --block-pad-t, which fits a title into 12.5px: and a 15-minute block at the agreed 12-hour default is 13.0px on the smallest supported display. So every block keeps a title at the default. Above 12 hours the user is explicitly trading labels for range.

Below the compact tier the title does go, and the question the ladder alone does not answer is how you read it then. Four mechanisms, in the order a user reaches for them:

1. Selection, into the detail panel. Click or [ enter ]. The full title, its Area, its origin, its binding and its reason. This is the primary answer and it is the same surface a full-size block uses, so there is one mechanism rather than two. Deliberately not a popover: a popover covers the grid it is explaining, dies on the next click, and would be a second mechanism for a job the panel already does. The inherited language's rule that no value is reachable only by hovering points the same way.

2. Zoom, to read a dense band in place. At 6 visible hours a 15-minute block is 26px and fully labeled.

3. The keyboard reaches every block at every tier. [ j ] and [ k ] do not care how tall a block is, so the smallest block in the week is exactly as reachable as the largest. That matters more than the pointer path, since a 9.8px pointer target is genuinely small.

4. The native title attribute, as a courtesy for the pointer. Never the only path to a value.

6 · The reason recordwhere the explanation comes from, and why the panel is rows rather than a paragraph

An earlier draft of the panel carried a hand-written sentence, and that was a design lie. It implied prose generation the product cannot do: the scheduler is deterministic and no language model sits in the placement loop, so a sentence that cannot be traced to a term the solver computed is worse than no sentence.

The solver emits a structured record, and the frontend renders it through a template catalogue. One template per clause kind, values substituted. Deterministic, unit-testable, translatable, and every clause traceable to a figure. Every clause kind below is something the solver already has to compute, so the reason is a projection of existing data rather than new machinery:
ClauseRenders asComes from
blockeda rejected candidate window and the constraint that rejected itthe hard-constraint checker, which already evaluates every candidate
dominantthe objective term with the largest share of total costthe objective breakdown, already stored on the plan revision
boundthe binding source and its cursor positionthe habit's binding source, already derived from the outcome log
flooran Area floor that forced or forbade the placementthe floor constraint, already a hard input to the solver
pinnedthe user's own edit, with the date it was madethe pin itself. the shortest reason in the system, and the strongest
Two consequences the design has to respect. The clause budget is bounded: the top two rejected windows plus the dominant term: because recording every candidate for ninety blocks a week would bloat the plan document, and the reason has to survive in an append-only store forever. And because the budget is bounded and the shape is fixed, the panel renders labeled rows rather than a paragraph, which is also denser and better suited to the register. Toggle the panel in section 1 to read it.

One question this record does not settle. A pinned block's reason is just "you put it here". Whether the panel should also show what the solver would have chosen, so the user can see the cost of their own pin, is a product question with a real storage cost: it means keeping the superseded placement alongside the pin. It is exactly the pairwise comparison the learning layer consumes, so the data may be worth keeping regardless.

7 · What this probe settlesand the one thing it hands to screens.html

Settled. Seven equal columns with the grid absorbing surplus width. A floor of 17 characters per day column in the single-line tier, derived from where real titles stop separating from their siblings, and every breakpoint derived from that floor rather than chosen. --bp-wide corrected from 1440px to 1536px, because the ledger showed 1440 with the panel open yields 15 characters. The 306px detail panel closing to a 26px rail rather than narrowing. Below --bp-compact the grid scrolls horizontally at --col-min per day with the axis sticky. The 64px summary strip at fixed height with its rows scrolling inside. The unconfirmed-day marker as a hollow ring on the day header: inline, informational, never a warning. Titles wrap to a line count computed per block. Reading a sub-label block is the detail panel's job, not a popover's.

Settled by replacing a rule rather than repairing it. Overlap uses the standard sweep-and-columns algorithm. A first draft special-cased the circadian frame as a full-width backdrop so a long Sleep span would not halve the day it backs; rendered, it was wrong twice over, because Wake Up is also origin frame yet is a genuine 15-minute participant, and because a full-width frame block painted over whatever it overlapped and clipped that block's title. Columns need no special case, depend on no paint order and use no magic threshold. Replaying the algorithm over the week confirms no block is ever covered, and that the widest cluster uses 3 columns, so the stagger path is never reached by real data.

One fixture correction, stated because it is not a design decision. The source week contained Sleep 00:00–09:00 on Thursday alongside a 07:00 lecture. That is a contradiction in the exported calendar, not a layout case: in the product a block inside the circadian frame is a conflict. The fixture now reads Sleep 00:00–06:45, Wake Up 06:45–07:00.

Handed forward. The strip carries three readings and the verdict. Whether those are the right three is a content question rather than a density one, and it belongs to screens.html where the weekly-session mode is drawn.
