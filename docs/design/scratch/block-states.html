1 · The channel mapnine meanings arrive at one rectangle. four are resolved at rest, the rest are moved off it

A calendar block differs from a transcript row in one way that drives everything else: it must be a bounded object distinct from the grid's own lines, so it spends a fill just to exist. The language syncr inherits gives hover the row fill, because a transcript row has no fill of its own. Here the fill channel takes three values instead, and they order correctly by accident of the palette: a block at --paper-raised is lighter than the page, hover at --ink-wash is darker, and a proposal has no fill at all. Absence of fill needs no new pigment and survives forced colors.

A block draws three edges, not four. The top rule carries the Area, the bottom rule closes the block at grid weight, the left rule is reserved for state, and there is no right or left border at all. A 1px horizontal inset keeps a block's own rules off the column divider. A split block is the one exception: it takes a left rule at --rule, because nothing else would divide it from its neighbor.

Conflict and selected share the left rule deliberately. They cannot co-occur meaningfully: a conflict is resolved by selecting it, at which point the detail panel states it in words. Conflict wins the pixel while it lasts. Section 5 renders that collision so it can be judged rather than argued about. Loudness, most to least: conflict, proposal, selected, focus, hover.

2 · The Area rampthe one break with one-ink. twelve pigments, three legal carriers, and a computed ledger

Every ratio below is computed in the browser from the linked tokens at load. An Area ink is a non-text indicator wherever it appears, so the bar is 3:1. The ramp is deliberately equalized: all twelve measure within 0.1 of 5.0:1 against page paper, because an uneven ramp makes some wedges read as heavier than others and biases the composition the pie review exists to show. The first draft ran 4.42:1 to 7.25:1 and the violet group looked like it held more time than the moss group at equal area. The 225° slot is vacant because that is cobalt's hue. Three pigments sit on the sealed signal hues by construction: 01 near oxide, 03 near amber, 06 near verdigris. That collision is accepted, and the carrier rule is what disambiguates it: a signal is never a fill, an Area is only ever a fill or a rule. The failure mode is honest and worth watching for: a madder Career block beside an oxide conflict rule will look related for the first week.
Hatch is not an accessibility toggle. It is always on, because color is never the only encoding. Six patterns is the honest ceiling: past that they stop separating at wedge scale, so a pattern repeats only between hues that are far apart. Each hatch is drawn in a lighter step of the wedge's own ink, so it reads as texture rather than as a second color. On ledger surfaces the chip pairs with the Area's name instead, which is stronger than any pattern. The tightest adjacent gaps are 02/03 at 19.4°, 06/07 and 07/08 at 20.3°, and 12/01 at 20.4°, which is uniform and about as tight as two dark colors can be and still separate. --pigment-area-07 was retuned from 183° to 176° to reach that: at 183° it sat 13.0° from 08 and the two were genuinely indistinguishable. The deal order does not defer these pairs: 07 and 08 are dealt 6th and 3rd: so what separates an adjacent pair is its hatch, and every one of the four tightest pairs carries a different one.

3 · A real day, every state in situa state has to be judged inside the grid, not as a floating rectangle. hover and tab are live

One realistic Tuesday, drawn from a real reference week: a gym rotation slot, an elastic queue-bound Leetcode, an external interview anchor with its declared prep and transit shadows, and the circadian frame as spans rather than markers. Blocks are positioned from their start minute and duration by script, so nothing here is placed by eye. Default zoom: 12 visible hours. Use the mouse and the Tab key.

The quarter hour is drawn at rest, because the snap is fifteen minutes and the grid should show where a block can land. Only the hour carries cobalt weight; the three quarters are --rule-faint. Press drag in progress and the quarters step up to hour weight, so the snap targets sharpen at the one moment the user is aiming at them. It is a discrete state change, not motion. A first draft drew --rule at every half hour plus --rule-faint at every quarter, while each block drew four --rule-strong edges: two cobalt lines an hour, and the blocks disappeared into the corduroy. Halving the cobalt fixed it. Deleting the subdivision was not necessary, and it was briefly deleted before this was understood.
Read the interview at 16:00 and the two shadows it casts. Prep for Kontron Placement Interview at 10:00 and Leave for Uni at 15:00 are generated from the anchor's declared type, not authored. The window from 16:45 to 18:00 is the recovery shadow, and it is now drawn: a hatched band in --rule with a gutter label, sitting under every block in z-order. It is deliberately not a block: it has no fill, no Area rule and no state, because it is the absence of a block that it exists to explain. The first render left it as nothing at all, which made a forbidden gap and an ordinary empty gap pixel-identical and the solver look like it was declining a gap for no reason.

5 · The combination matrixevery combination of the five row-level channels. this is the view that rejects a system

Validate every state in combination, never in isolation. In the language syncr inherits, four plausible state systems each looked correct on a single state and produced two pixel-identical rows here while meaning different things. Read down this list looking for any two rows you cannot tell apart. Rows 14 and 15 are the deliberate collision: conflict and selected sharing the left rule.

Rows 19 to 25 are the pairs section 15's own combination table settles and this matrix did not render. Each is a channel shared with something: the pin glyph against the oxide rule, the split's 1px rule against the 3px state rule, the origin glyph against the pin glyph at the sliver tier, and the anchor's hatch against both the oxide rule and the hover fill. The anchor pair is the one to read closely: hatch is a background IMAGE and hover is a background COLOR, so hovering an anchor steps the fill beneath the texture rather than erasing it. Had both spent background, hovering would have deleted the one mark that says the product does not own that time.

6 · The proposal pair, and the discrete draga proposal moves an existing block, so the honest rendering shows both ends

Proposal · the solver wants to move Gym from 05:30 to 13:00
What each end carries
source, 05:30keeps its fill. takes → in the glyph slot
target, 13:00no fill. the Area's own top rule survives, the bottom rule dashes to --ink-deep, text --ink-soft
notificationnone. a proposal waits quietly and is visible when the user looks

Dashed was the obvious carrier and it was rejected. A dashed border already means disabled on a control, and one visual carrying two meanings is what this system forbids. Absence of fill is unclaimed, unambiguous, and survives forced colors: where a fill is dropped anyway, leaving the dashed outline as the only surviving difference. That is why the target keeps a dashed outline as well: in forced colors the missing fill stops being a signal.

7 · Overlapeven split to depth 3, then stagger. a 186px column is the budget

Overlap is expected: a user multitasks, and the product does not pretend otherwise. Even split up to depth 3, so the common case is exact. Past 3, stagger with the later start on top at a fixed --overlap-indent, plus a count marker in the frontmost block, because an even fifth of a column leaves nothing to write in. A split block takes a left rule at --rule, because with no right or left border of its own nothing else would divide it from its neighbor. Character budgets at a 143px day column, --fs-block 11.5px: depth 1 is 18 characters, depth 2 is 8, depth 3 is 5.

8 · The tier ladder, and the record of what this probe settledthe same four blocks at four zoom levels

A block's height is proportional to its duration, so at high zoom a 15 minute block falls below the height its own title needs. The block degrades; the axis never lies. Below --block-h-label the title goes and the origin glyph stays. Below --block-h-sliver only the bounded sliver and the Area rule survive. The title is always available on selection. Each panel below prints its own pixels-per-minute and the tier a 15 minute block lands in, computed from the linked tokens, so the ladder can be checked rather than believed.
◆ What this probe settles

Settled by rendering: the channel map in section 1, the three-value fill channel, the proposal pair as absence-of-fill, the 12-ink ramp with three sealed carriers, even-split-to-3-then-stagger, the tier ladder, and the block title at --fs-chrome with a 19px floor.

Settled in review, from the rendered output rather than from description: the grid draws the hour only, in --rule-faint, and reveals the quarter-hour subdivision only during a drag. A block draws three edges, not four. The hour label knocks out its own background. The zoom range clamps per display so a thirty-minute block always keeps its label. The recovery shadow is a hatched band, not an absence. Three adjacent Area top rules at depth 3 read as three Areas.

Settled in review · the block title is --fs-chrome 11.5px, and the floor is 19px. The first draft used 9.5px to keep the floor at 15px, which let a fifteen-minute block hold a title at more zoom levels. Rendered, it was unreadable at the size the Week screen is used at, and it made the product's primary content the smallest type in the system. The second render then showed the title jammed against the Area rule, so --block-pad-t is 2px and the floor is the sum of what has to fit: 2 rule + 2 pad + 13.8 line + 1 rule = 18.8px inside 19px. Padding is asymmetric: zero at the bottom, because a title is top-aligned and symmetry would cost another zoom step. The cost is paid by the fifteen-minute tier, which now slivers at the 12 hour default on anything smaller than a 27-inch. Accepted: those blocks are the circadian frame and are read from position. The consequence that still needs your ruling is the zoom cap. To keep a thirty-minute block labeled: the modal duration by a wide margin: the 6–24 range must clamp at 16h on a 13-inch, 22h on a 16-inch, 24h on a 27-inch. Accept the clamp, or allow the full 24 everywhere and let the modal block sliver too?
Settled · the zoom range clamps per display. To keep a thirty-minute block labeled: the modal duration by a wide margin: the 6–24 range caps at 16h on a 13-inch, 22h on a 16-inch, 24h on a 27-inch. The cap is derived from the grid height and --block-h-label, not stored. A zoom level at which the most common block in the system is unreadable is not offered.
Settled · the rotation cursor is derived, not editable. Gym is on Legs because Chest & Back was confirmed completed, so the cursor is a projection of the append-only outcome log rather than a field. It is displayed read-only with that provenance. A wrong cursor means a wrong confirmation, corrected on Today, after which the cursor re-derives. Overriding a single occurrence is a rebind, which is a pin, which already exists. No new concept and no desync possible by construction.
Settled · three adjacent top rules at depth 3 read as three Areas. Section 7 renders it. The one-carrier-per-context rule therefore holds without an exception: the top rule stays the sole Area carrier on a proportional surface at every overlap depth.

9 · Forced colorsthe OS drops every fill. what survives is what the state actually is

Toggle the simulation in section 3, then re-read sections 4, 5 and 7. Every state except hover survives, because each pairs its fill with an outline, a border or a glyph. Hover does not survive, and that is acceptable: it is mouse-only and nothing depends on it to operate the product. The Area ramp does not survive either, which is precisely why hatch and the Area name are structural rather than optional.
