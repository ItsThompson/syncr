1 · Does a pinned block show what the solver would have chosen? : settled, and it obliges the schema

A pinned block's reason is just you put it here, which is true and unhelpful. Showing the superseded placement lets you see the cost of your own pin. It has a real storage cost: the rejected placement has to be kept alongside the pin, forever, in an append-only store. It is also exactly the pairwise comparison the learning layer consumes, so the data is worth keeping whether or not the panel shows it. Chosen: show it. The superseded placement and its objective delta are now persisted permanently alongside every pin, which is recorded in the design language and in the PRD’s entity table rather than only here.

2 · Grid line density at rest : settled, recorded here

A first render drew --rule every half hour and --rule-faint every quarter, while each block also drew four --rule-strong edges. Four lines an hour, two at cobalt weight, and the blocks disappeared into the corduroy. Chosen: every quarter hour, because the snap is fifteen minutes and the grid should show where a block can land. The remedy for the busy render was not to delete the subdivision: it was to halve the cobalt. The hour alone carries --rule, the three quarters carry --rule-faint, and a block draws three edges instead of four. Same line count as the version that was rejected, half the cobalt weight. During a drag the quarters step up to hour weight so the snap targets sharpen.

3 · Which channel carries the Area on a block : settled, recorded here

The 12-ink Area ramp is the one sealed exception to one ink, so where it lands matters more than usual. Chosen: the 2px top rule, because the left rule is already spoken for by conflict and selected, and because at the sliver tier the top rule is the only thing that survives.

4 · How the circadian frame is drawn : settled

Routines are not budgeted as discretionary time, so the frame takes no Area ink. It still has to read as structure rather than as content.

5 · The pinned mark : settled, recorded here

A pin is the user's own edit and the strongest reason in the system, so it has to be visible at rest. Chosen: a glyph in the glyph slot, because it survives forced colors as text and costs no geometry.

6 · The deviation bar's zero : settled

Actual against target per Area. Cobalt only: Area ink encodes identity, cobalt encodes magnitude and direction, and direction is a sign rather than a hue.

7 · The Area chip's shape : settled

Radius is zero everywhere except a short allowlist of genuinely circular things: status dot, radio dot, avatar. Is an Area chip one of them?

8 · What the pinned summary strip carries : settled

The strip is fixed at --strip-h and its rows scroll inside, because a panel that grew as pins accumulate would shift the grid under the cursor. So its content is a fixed budget, and the third reading competes with per-Area bars.
