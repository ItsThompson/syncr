The spec block beside each component is the point of this sheet. It names the component, the exact tokens it consumes, the attributes and pseudo-classes that drive its states, and the rule that would be broken by building it any other way. Every state driver is a CSS pseudo-class or a data-* attribute, so nothing here needs a class toggled by script to be styleable. Read the token files first: frontend/src/tokens/ carries the reasoning, and this sheet carries the consequences. Where a component looks awkward, the awkwardness is usually a locked decision doing its job, and it is stated rather than designed around.

07 · Severity: four volumes and four pigmentsvolume is how loudly the product interrupts. pigment is what kind of thing happened

Severity is two independent axes, and the mistake every alert system makes is collapsing them into one. The pigment says what kind of thing happened: --ink-bright informational, --signal-amber a notice, --signal-oxide a failure, --signal-verdigris resolved. The volume says how loudly the product interrupts: inline, panel, banner, blocking. A conflict is oxide and loud; a week that cannot hold its commitments is amber and quiet, because it is a notice about the future rather than a failure in the present. The pigment never becomes a fill: it lives in a 3px rule, a glyph and a label. The one sanctioned signal surface in the system is --amber-wash under the infeasibility panel, which color.css names for exactly that purpose.

Volume 4, blocking, is drawn once here and is deliberately unused. syncr never blocks approval of a knowingly broken week: it raises, warns and allows. A product that refuses the approve action when the user has knowingly overcommitted has decided something that belongs to the user, and the user is the one who knows they are tired. The volume is documented so that the absence is a decision on the record rather than an omission.

The sidebar is paper, and that is a deliberate break with the language syncr inherits, which fills its sidebar with --ink-deep. Rendered on a screen whose whole point is a calm grid, an ink sidebar dominated it, and it made the one permanently visible surface the loudest thing in the product. The inherited language's own rule points the same way: the loudest treatment is not spent on the most persistent condition. So the sidebar is paper with a --rule-strong right edge, and the current item takes a wash plus a 3px --ink-deep left rule: the same channel a selected block uses. One idea, one form.

Two consequences worth naming. Because no surface here is an ink fill, the standard focus ring is correct throughout and this component needs no inverse-ring scope, which is the defect that cost the inherited language its keyboard ring on its primary navigation. And because the current item is a wash plus a rule rather than a fill, the seven counts stay --text-muted on every row including the current one: there is no second surface to re-measure them against. Counts sit on Backlog, Proposals and Unconfirmed only. A count on Week or Settings would be a number with no question behind it, and a badge that never changes is furniture.

12 · Blockthe calendar block. four tiers, nine states, and the .blk rules are verbatim from the probes

The .blk CSS in this sheet is copied verbatim from scratch/week-density.html, which took it verbatim from scratch/block-states.html. Three sheets, one block. The pinned and proposal rules are the two week-density.html does not carry, ported from block-states.html unchanged. Where a component sheet and a probe disagree about the block, the probe is right, and the correct fix is to re-copy rather than to reconcile by hand.

A block spends a fill just to exist, which is the one way it differs from every row in the system: it must be a bounded object distinct from the grid's own lines. So the fill channel has three values and they order correctly by accident of the palette: --paper-raised is lighter than the page, hover at --ink-wash is darker, and a proposal has no fill at all. A block draws three edges, not four: the top rule carries the Area, the bottom rule closes it at grid weight, the left rule is reserved for state, and there is no right border. A split block is the one exception and takes a left rule at --rule, because with no side borders nothing else would divide it from its neighbor.
The four tiers, drawn at each tier's own floor. Heights and line counts are computed from the tokens, not typed
The floor is the sum of what has to fit, not a round number. Label tier: 2px Area rule + 2px --block-pad-t + 13.8px of line + 1px bottom rule = 18.8px inside a 19px floor. Compact tier: 2 + 0 + 9.5 + 1 = 12.5px inside 13px, paid for by giving up the top padding so the title sits against the Area rule. The compact tier exists because the three-tier ladder was rejected in review: an unlabeled Wake Up reads as a rendering fault rather than as a deliberate tier. The hairline tier is unreachable on any supported display given the zoom clamp, and is kept as a defensive floor. The title wraps to a line count computed from the block's own height, so a clip lands on a line boundary: a measured ledger over one real week showed nowrap made 4 of 37 titles ambiguous, because end-ellipsis discards exactly the tail that separates Amazon Interview Prep from Amazon Interview Prep · Behavioral.
In situ · two real days at the 12 hour default. Overlap is resolved by the sweep-and-columns algorithm from week-density.html, unchanged. Hover and Tab are live
Every state above is judged in the grid, not as a floating rectangle. Tuesday carries the conflict on the 16:00 anchor, the recovery shadow it casts from 16:45 to 18:00 as a hatched band, the now rule at 14:20, the pin on Gym · Shoulder & Arms and the selection on Leetcode · Graphs. Friday carries the real three-way overlap at 11:30, which is where data-split comes from: the split state is produced by the layout algorithm, never authored. Replaying that algorithm over the reference week shows the widest cluster uses three columns, so the stagger path past --overlap-max-split is never reached by real data. The quarter hour is drawn at rest, because the snap is fifteen minutes and the grid should show where a block can land. Only the hour carries cobalt weight; the three quarters are --rule-faint. During a drag the quarters step up to hour weight so the snap targets sharpen, which is a discrete state change rather than motion, and the drag itself moves a hairline marker rather than the block.

17 · ReasonRowsthe explainability surface. labeled rows, never a paragraph

An earlier draft of the detail panel carried a hand-written sentence, and that was a design lie. It implied prose generation the product cannot do: the scheduler is deterministic and no language model sits in the placement loop, so a sentence that cannot be traced to a term the solver computed is worse than no sentence at all. The solver emits a structured record and the frontend renders it through a template catalogue, one template per clause kind with values substituted. Deterministic, unit-testable, translatable, and every clause traceable to a figure. Section 6 of scratch/week-density.html is the record of that decision.

There are six clause kinds: blocked, dominant, bound, floor, pinned and instead of. The last one is new, and it settles the open question week-density.html handed forward: a pinned block now also states the placement the pin overrode, with the objective delta the pin cost. It is worth its storage, because the superseded placement is exactly the pairwise comparison the learning layer consumes: the user chose 05:30 when the solver proposed 13:00, in this context. The editing interaction and the training-data collection are the same interaction, so the panel is showing the user the record it is already keeping about them.

Two consequences the component has to respect. The clause budget is bounded: the top two rejected windows, the dominant objective term, and on a pinned block the one superseded placement. Recording every candidate for ninety blocks a week would bloat a plan document that has to survive in an append-only store forever. And because the budget is bounded and the shape is fixed, rows are the correct form rather than a paragraph: they are denser, they align, and they suit the register. A paragraph would also invite an author to smooth over a missing clause with a conjunction.

Block characters are correct here because the scale is bounded. Unlock progress runs 0 to 100% and has a known end, so a segmented run reads as a fraction of something. An unbounded magnitude would need a percentage-width fill instead, because block characters offer roughly fourteen steps at this size and a range above 20:1 would reduce every non-leading row to a one-cell stub. This is not a progress bar and it does not advance while you watch it. It moves when a confirmed day is added, which is a discrete redraw of a number.

Maturity is per parameter, not one global flag, because parameters converge at very different rates: a duration multiplier needs 10 to 15 confirmed blocks and lands around week 2, while the objective weights need 50 to 100 edit events and land around month 3. Unlocks are counted on the volume of confirmed days, never on adherence. A day where the user skipped everything and said so counts exactly as much as a perfect day: tying unlocks to adherence would reward marking things done, and the integrity of the whole dataset rests on honest confirmation. A parameter still collecting baseline is informational and never a warning, which is why the row's dot is --ink-bright: it is the expected state of a new install. The value column shows the prior in that case, and says so, rather than printing a fitted number nobody fitted.

19 · LedgerRowToday. time, duration right-aligned and tabular, Area chip, outcome controls

This is the surface that owns reality state, and it is the reason a block does not. A calendar block carries nine meanings already; adding presumed, completed, partial and skipped to it would need a tenth channel it does not have. So reality moves here, where a row has room for a dot, a word and a control. Blocks are presumed completed unless the user says otherwise, which keeps the daily interaction cost near zero, and the evening pass converts presumed to recorded for the whole day at once with [ c ].

Partial minutes use a 5-minute stepper pre-filled with the block's planned duration. Pre-filled, because the overwhelmingly common correction is slightly less than planned and an empty field would make the honest answer more work than the flattering one. Five minutes rather than the 15-minute --snap the plan uses, because a recorded actual is a measurement, not a plan: the snap exists so that starts and ends land on the grid, and nothing about a measurement lands on a grid. partial with actual minutes is the sole source of the duration-estimate signal, so a stepper that discouraged its use would cost the learning layer its best input. The Area chip is the ledger carrier at 10px, always beside the Area's name, and the anchor row has neither, because an anchor belongs to no Area: it is time the product does not own.

The bracket pair comes from the glyph table, which states it once, so the product has one bracketed-glyph form rather than two. The plan is correct. syncr owns the plan and the calendar is a projection of it, so nothing has been lost: reconnecting rewrites all 91 blocks. Until then your phone is showing a week that no longer exists.

There is no spinner and no skeleton loader anywhere in this product, and the pending state above is what replaces them. A hairline placeholder holds the row height so the layout does not jump when the rows arrive, and the count underneath is the actual reading: it changes when a number changes, which is a discrete redraw rather than motion. --radius is 0 and the 50% allowlist covers four elements, none of which is a spinner, so the usual spinner cannot even be drawn in this language. The language syncr inherits kept one motion exception for a cold-start index that took 4.6 seconds; syncr has no such wait, because a solve over roughly thirty blocks a day is instantaneous.

The empty state's headline is mono, not the serif. The serif appears in exactly two places: the wordmark and the seven page titles. An empty-state headline is neither. The error state states what is still true before it states the fix, because the alarming reading of a failed calendar write is that the plan is gone, and it is not: syncr owns the plan and the calendar is a projection. An error message that leaves the user unsure whether their data survived has failed even if it is accurate.
Provenance. Every color, size, height, weight and rule on this sheet resolves from frontend/src/tokens/index.css at load: the sheet links the same file the app imports, so a value here cannot drift from the build, and there is not one hardcoded color in it. Content comes from the same real reference week the two probes use, Mon 10 to Sun 16 February, 91 blocks and 80.8h scheduled. Figures that appear on more than one component are computed once and rendered from the same numbers, so the card in section 06, the deviation bars in section 14, the wedges in section 15 and the verdict in section 16 cannot disagree: 52.1h discretionary, 33.7h scheduled into Areas, 18.4h the solver could not place. Block tier heights, line counts and the overlap columns are computed from the tokens and by the same sweep-and-columns algorithm the probes use, so nothing on section 12 is placed by eye.

Two values on this sheet were not tokens when it was written, and both now are. The dialog scrim in section 10 is --scrim, a translucent veil derived from --ink-deep rather than a new pigment, so the page stays legible beneath it. The hatch inks on the wedges and stacked bars in section 15 read --hatch-mix, the single step by which a hatch is lightened from the fill it sits on, written as color-mix(in srgb, var(--ai) var(--hatch-mix), var(--paper-raised)) against each wedge’s own Area token. Both were promoted because more than one sheet had re-derived them inline, which is how two references drift apart.
